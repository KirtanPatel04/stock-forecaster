# Ross Cameron day-trading method

Distilled from Ross Cameron's own videos (the "$2,000 → $105,951 in 46 days" and "$2k → $65k in 30 days" strategy classes, "Stock Selection in a Hot vs Cold Market", and his daily recaps and max-loss days, Aug–Sep 2026). Sources are in [references/sources.md](references/sources.md).

His framework has three steps, in this order:
1. **Stock selection**: find the most obvious stock in the market.
2. **Entry**: wait for the first pullback, then buy the first candle that makes a new high.
3. **Exit**: sell on exit indicators, and know when to stop trading for the day.

Risk management comes before all three. Always say this: day trading is risky, his results are not typical, and he tells beginners to prove the method in a simulator before using real money.

---

## Step 1: Stock selection (the Five Pillars)

The first four pillars measure **demand**. The fifth measures **supply**. A big move (+100–300%) needs demand to overwhelm a small supply.

| # | Pillar | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | % change | **Up ≥ 10%** on the day (A-quality: ≥ 30%) | Proves demand is already there. He trades the top 2–3 **leading gainers**. |
| 2 | Relative volume | **≥ 5× the 50-day average volume**. He calls this a must. | Most of his 10-year profit comes from stocks with 5× relative volume or more. Low relative volume was his #1 listed mistake. |
| 3 | News catalyst | **Fresh breaking news today** (earnings, FDA, contract, partnership, offering news, etc.) | Explains *why* the stock has demand. Most catalysts drop after hours or pre-market, so he prefers stocks **gapping up** (> 2%). |
| 4 | Price | **$2–$20**. **$5–$10 is the sweet spot.** | Popular with retail traders, fits small accounts. He has done poorly above $20. |
| 5 | Float | **< 20M shares** (A-quality: **< 10M**) | Low supply means violent moves. Leading gainers almost always have small floats. |

### Extra filters for A-quality setups
- **Time of day: 7:00–10:00 AM ET.** His profits fall off steadily after the open, and 7:00–9:30 pre-market is the cleanest. After 9:30, market orders, stop orders and halts give market makers the edge. Afternoon trades leave no time to recover from a mistake.
- **Volume today > 25M shares** does better than thin volume. Don't *anticipate* volume, wait for it to confirm.
- **Obviousness:** is this the stock *everyone* is watching (the #1 leading gainer)? A 28% gainer that is the sixth-best gainer on the day isn't obvious.
- **Theme / sympathy:** after a huge runner (for example a Chinese small cap up 2,000%), traders pile into the next similar stock (same country, sector, recent reverse split plus news). Treat a stock that fits the theme as more obvious.
- **Exchange:** be careful with NYSE/NYSE American (AMEX) runners after the open. Those exchanges halt big movers more readily and hold them longer.
- **Daily chart:** note nearby resistance such as the 200-day moving average, and a history of spiking then giving the gains back.
- **Short squeezes:** a stock with no news can still squeeze when trapped short sellers have to cover. If it's the leading gainer, don't ignore it. Trade it with **less risk** because it only meets 4 of 5 pillars.
- **Warning flags:** a secondary offering filed right after the news, a big hidden seller, or heavy dilution history.

### Grading
- **A+**: all 5 pillars, plus up ≥ 30%, price $5–10, float < 10M, and inside the 7–10 AM window.
- **A**: all 5 pillars.
- **B**: 4 of 5. Tradable only with **reduced size**, and only in a hot market.
- **C**: 3 or fewer. Watch only. The one exception is a clean chart in a hot market: read the price action, not just the listed float (a low float that trades choppy is still untradable).

### Hot vs cold market
- **Hot** (several stocks up 50–100%+ on 5× relative volume, runners follow through): you can be *slightly* looser (4/5 pillars). Take your foot off the gas quickly when it cools. The cool-down usually arrives as a big loss on a stock that didn't meet the pillars.
- **Cold** (few gainers, breakouts fail): be strict (5/5 only), use a smaller "cold-market daily goal", trade less, walk away sooner. Beginners should treat the pillars as black and white.

## Step 2: Entry (the first pullback)

**Never buy the middle of a squeeze.** Your stop would be at the last support, far below, and the risk would be bigger than the reward. Instead:

1. Let the stock squeeze up on the scanner. Watch it, don't chase it.
2. Wait for the **first pullback**. It should:
   - retrace **no more than ~50%** of the squeeze,
   - have **light volume on the red candles** and heavy volume on the green ones,
   - **hold above VWAP**,
   - **hold above the 9 EMA** (on the 1-minute chart),
   - ideally form a bottoming tail and no big topping tails.
3. **Entry trigger:** the **first candle to make a new high** (the "crossing candle", where one candle breaks over the prior candle's high). Buy as it breaks.
4. **Stop (max loss):** the **low of the pullback**.
5. **Target:** first a retest of the **high of day**, then continuation. Take half at the high of day if you're being conservative. Only add in a hot market.
6. **Is it worth taking?** The potential reward must be at least the risk (target 2:1). He usually captures *less than half* of the potential he sees, so a setup offering only $0.10/share isn't worth taking.

If a stock meets every pillar but never forms a clean pattern, **no trade**. Other patterns he uses (micro pullbacks on the 10-second chart, pre-market high breaks, bull flags) are more advanced. Master the first pullback first ("play only a few hands").

## Step 3: Exits

### Exit indicators (sell on the first clear one, or several together)
1. A **big seller** on Level 2 (for example 100k shares on the ask above an extended stock).
2. A **hidden seller**: lots of buying on the tape but the price won't move.
3. A **burst of red on the tape** (a surge of selling, often a false breakout).
4. A **pop then a sharp reversal**, leaving a topping tail.
5. **Buying slowing** on time & sales.
6. A **topping tail, doji, or first red candle** at the top of the move. Volume falling while price rises (divergence) is also a warning.

Get in and get out. He doesn't try to be the hero who holds through a round trip. **Respect the stop.** His two biggest self-reported mistakes are holding losers too long and cutting winners too soon, and both come from trading too big a size.

### When to walk away for the day
- You've **given back half** of the day's peak profit.
- You've hit your **max daily loss**.
- You've had **3 losing trades in a row** (small-account rule).
- The **7–10 AM window** has closed.
- There are **no A-quality setups** left.
- The **market is cold**, or today's theme is bearish.
- Once you stop, **stop watching**. Checking the phone leads to fear of missing out, revenge trades and a worse red day.

## Risk & position sizing: your daily goal in 2–3 trades

**Ask the user first:** how much real cash they have, and **how much they want to make per day**. Suggest ~5% of the account (Ross's $2k challenge started around $100/day). Flag anything over 10% as very aggressive, because the max daily loss equals the goal. Then ask whether they want to reach it in **2 or 3 winning trades**.

| Setting | Rule |
|---|---|
| Daily goal | the user's $ number (default suggestion: 5% of the account) |
| Per-trade goal | `daily goal ÷ trades (2 or 3)`, which is what each winner should pay |
| Risk per trade | `per-trade goal ÷ 2`, because **every trade is 2:1** |
| Max daily loss | **= daily goal** (his max loss equals his daily goal) |
| Shares | `min( floor(risk per trade ÷ (entry − stop)), floor(buying power ÷ entry) )`, so the 2:1 target pays the per-trade goal. If cash caps the size, say how many winners they'd need instead. |
| Target | always `entry + 2 × (entry − stop)`, recomputed from the **actual fill** |
| Ross's cushion rule (opt-in) | ¼ size until ¼ of the goal is banked |
| Consecutive losers | **3 in a row: done for the day** |
| After a max-loss day | reduce share size. Recover with accuracy, not size. |

*Example:* $2,000 account, $100/day in 2 trades: each winner pays **+$50**, each loser costs **−$25**, stop for the day at **−$100**. With a 5¢ stop that's 500 shares, capped by cash.

**Canadian (CAD) accounts.** US stocks trade in USD, but the user's cash, goal and P&L are in CAD.
- Convert at the **live USD→CAD rate**. For real trades, use the rate their broker actually used.
- Size so the 2:1 winner pays the per-trade goal **in CAD after converting back**.
- Ask about the broker's **FX fee per conversion** (often ~1.5% each way; 0 if they hold USD or use Norbert's gambit). Fees come out of every winner and add to every loser. At 1.5% each way a small-cap 2:1 on a few-cent move is often **worse than 1:1 in CAD**, so flag it, and pass when after-fee R:R < 1. Recommend a USD sub-account for day trading.
- A trade's CAD P&L includes the FX move between buy and sell.
- The US pattern-day-trader rule doesn't apply at Canadian brokers.

**Real vs. paper.** The app never places orders. When the user records a buy or sell, always ask whether it was a **real order with their broker** (then ask for the **actual fill price and share count**) or a **paper trade**. Real trades move their cash; paper trades don't. Keep them separable in any review.

**Accuracy first.** At 1:1 profit/loss you break even at 50% accuracy. He runs about 70%. Accuracy builds confidence, confidence allows more size, and more size brings more profit. Raise size only after green days and green weeks.

**Account notes:** his 2026 challenge ran in a **cash account** (Schwab, no leverage), so buying power equaled cash. With a cash account, funds settle T+1 and pattern day trader (PDT) rules don't apply. A **margin account under $25k** is limited by PDT to 3 day trades in 5 days. Commission-free brokers are fine up to about $25–50k. Above that, a direct-access broker (he uses Lightspeed) gives faster fills.

## Realistic targets: 2:1 within about 30 minutes

Day trades are fast: get in, get out. A target only counts if **this stock, the way it's trading today**, can realistically reach it in about **30 minutes**. So the stop is sized to fit the stock's speed. Often that's a few cents, not a dollar.

1. **Measure its speed.** Over the last ~2 hours of 1-minute bars, take each bar's close and find the best high reached in the next 30 bars.
   - The **median** of those run-ups is the *typical 30-minute run*.
   - The **75th percentile** is a *good run*.
   - Also note the average 1-minute candle range, which tells you how much normal noise there is.
2. **Size the stop to fit that speed.** Risk per share = the tighter of:
   - **half the typical 30-minute run**, so 2R fits inside a typical run, and
   - the **pullback low**, Ross's structural stop.

   The stop can **never be tighter than ½ an average 1-minute candle** (and at least 1 tick). Tighter than that, normal wiggles stop you out.

   *Example:* a $3.78 stock that typically runs 8¢ in 30 minutes, with 5¢ candles, gets a 3¢ stop and a **2:1 target 6¢ above entry, about 17 minutes away**.
3. **Target = entry + 2R.** Rate it:
   - **Likely:** a typical 30-minute run covers it.
   - **Possible:** it needs a good run, or a new high above the high of day.
   - **Stretch:** it's more than the stock moves in 30 minutes, or beyond half the first squeeze leg past the high of day. **Stretch means PASS: not a fast trade.**

   The estimated time is `30 min × (distance ÷ typical run)²`, because price moves scale with √time.
4. **Scale out** at 2:1. Only hold a runner toward 3:1 or the high-of-day retest while no exit indicator shows up.
5. **Share count:** tight stops mean more shares by risk, so size is usually **capped by cash**. That's fine: the dollars at risk shrink too.

## Today's game plan: picking the trades that hit the goal

At the start of the prime window (**7:00 AM ET**) and again at the **9:30 open**, pick the stocks most likely to get the user to their daily goal:

1. Start from the live scan plus anything they starred on last night's watchlist.
2. **Drop every PASS:** broken or stale pattern, ≤3 pillars, a B-grade stock in a cold market, a 2:1 target that's a stretch within ~30 minutes, a position cash can't size, or a session guard that says stop.
3. **Rank what's left:**
   - live setup first (BUY > READY > WAIT)
   - then grade (A+ > A > B)
   - then how realistic 2:1 is (likely > possible)
   - then how much of the per-trade goal the position pays with their cash
   - then leading-gainer rank and a fresh catalyst
4. **Present picks in order:** the top *N* (N = trades per goal), labeled "trade 1, trade 2, …", plus a couple of backups. Each pick shows shares, entry trigger, stop, the 2:1 target and $ at target.
5. **Be honest about the gaps:**
   - If cash caps the picks below the goal, say how many winners they'd actually need.
   - If fewer stocks qualify than trades needed, say "don't force the rest."
   - If nothing qualifies: "waiting is a position."

## Verdict: should I buy it?

Combine every factor into one call. The first rule that applies decides it:

| Verdict | When |
|---|---|
| **PASS** | Session guard says stop · grade C (≤3 pillars) · grade B in a cold market · past 10 AM and not A+ · outside the window (afternoon) · pattern broken or stale · 2:1 target isn't reachable in ~30 min (stretch) or reward < risk · cash can't buy at least ¼ of the risk-based size |
| **WAIT** | Extended (still squeezing, don't chase) · no setup yet · before 7 AM · market closed (put it on tomorrow's watchlist) · cash not entered |
| **READY** | Everything lines up but the trigger hasn't fired. "Buy only if it breaks $X, stop $Y, 2:1 target $Z." |
| **BUY** | Everything lines up **and** the first candle to make a new high is breaking now |

Always list the reasons for and against, plus a size note: reduced size for grade B, ¼ size until the cushion is built.

## After hours → next day (the watchlist)

From his "Day Trading Watch List for Monday" and "Top 2 Stocks on Watch" episodes:

1. **After the close (4–8 PM ET):** pull up the **after-hours top gainers** and the day's leading gainers. News that drops after hours drives the next morning's gappers.
2. **Check each name** in this order: float, price, relative volume, **country**, catalyst, then the **daily chart** (200-day MA overhead? a history of spiking and fading?).
3. **Cross off / discount:**
   - **Buyouts:** the stock jumps, then trades dead flat. Its value has been fixed, so it has nowhere to go.
   - **No-news Chinese / HK / Singapore / Malaysia small caps:** many are pumped through WhatsApp/Telegram groups while insiders dump. Be skeptical and use small size.
   - **Closed red / well off the high:** "don't get married to it."
   - **Continuation of a day-one runner:** these only work in a hot market.
   - **Serial spike-and-faders:** expect sellers into strength.
4. **Plus points:** a **recent reverse split with fresh news** (tiny float, a theme that has worked), a recent IPO, and lower float than a similar stock with the same volume ("same demand, less supply").
5. **Calendar:** **Mondays** have been his best day (companies release good news early in the week). **Fridays** are weaker (bad news gets buried there). Near **quarter-end**, expect offerings that knock runners back.
6. **Next morning, 6:45–7:00 AM:** re-check the **pre-market top gainers**. Last night's list only matters if the stock is still in play. The **pre-market high** is the key level. Then go to the 1-minute chart and wait for the first pullback. **Don't chase the open.**

Watchlist tiers: **TOP** (5/5 pillars on the after-hours / pre-market move), **WATCH** (4/5, needs pre-market confirmation), **LOW** (3/5 or fewer), **SKIP** (buyout or a hard fail).

## How to apply this skill

### When the user asks "what should I day trade?" / "find me stocks"
1. If the local app is running, pull the live scan:
   `curl -s "http://localhost:8000/api/daytrade/scan" | python3 -m json.tool`
   (single ticker: `/api/daytrade/stock/{SYMBOL}`). Otherwise use web search for today's top small-cap gainers and check each against the pillars.
2. Ask for (or reuse) their **cash**, **account type**, **daily $ goal** and whether they want to reach it in **2 or 3 trades**. Compute the per-trade goal, risk per trade, max loss and share size with the table above.
3. Output a ranked list, **BUY and READY first, then most obvious**. For each stock give the **verdict (BUY / READY / WAIT / PASS) with the reasons**, the grade, which pillars pass or fail, the pullback state, entry, stop, the **2:1 target and how realistic it is**, shares for their cash, $ at risk and $ at target.
4. Say plainly when there's **nothing worth trading** (cold market, no A-quality setups, or outside the time window). "No trade" is a valid answer.
5. End with the risk reminder and the walk-away rules for the session.

### When the user asks "what should I buy to hit my goal today?"
Use the game-plan ranking above on the live scan (`/api/daytrade/scan`) with their cash, daily goal and trades-per-goal. Give the numbered picks with shares, trigger, stop, 2:1 target and $ at target, and list what was excluded and why.

### When the user asks "what should I watch tomorrow?"
Pull `curl -s "http://localhost:8000/api/daytrade/watchlist"` (add `?tickers=A,B` to force-include names) and present the TOP/WATCH names with the move (after-hours or pre-market), float, catalyst, key levels (after-hours / pre-market high, high of day, 200 MA) and the plan for 7 AM. Flag every cross-off reason from the section above.

### When reviewing a trade or a red day
Check it against the method: Was it 5/5 pillars? Was the entry on the first pullback or a chase? Was the stop at the pullback low? Was size right for the cushion? Did they ignore an exit indicator? Did they break a walk-away rule (gave back half, 3 losers, traded past 10 AM)? Be direct, the way he is in his own recaps.

### Live levels
Entry, stop and the 2:1 target are recomputed from the 1-minute chart every ~5 s (`frontend/src/lib/liveSetup.ts`, a port of the backend's `analyze_setup`). Levels the user types in stay frozen until they switch back to live. Positions keep the levels they were opened with.
